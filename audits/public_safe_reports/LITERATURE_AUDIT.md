# Q587 source and prior-work audit

Checked November 27, 2026. **High confidence** in the offline target and distinction from the earlier hardness results below. **Moderate confidence** that the common receiver utility difference, two actions, and state-independent sender utility together are outside the inspected prior hardness theorems. The search does not establish absolute priority.

## Exact target

Vurankaya and Topcu's July 30, 2026 arXiv v1 gives a known-channel offline model in Section 2, equations (2) and (3). A full-support state prior is given, the sender controls only the sender message, and the private receiver signal is conditionally independent of that message given the state. The sender and receiver utility tables depend on action and state. The receiver has two actions, and receiver ties favor the sender. The Remark after Lemma 6 asks whether the offline computation is polynomial-time for binary actions. The number of private signals is an input parameter and can exceed two. The presented hardness is for rational data in this unrestricted offline formulation. Assumption 1 and the margin constraint (20b) are introduced for online reconstruction from estimated hyperplanes. The reduction uses weak incentives and does not impose that margin or make a regret claim. [Pinned Q587](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/game_theory/games-of-incomplete-information-bayesian-games.md#q587) · [Vurankaya and Topcu, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28342v1#Sx2.E3) · [Compute-Signaling remark](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28342v1#Sx4.SSx5)

For a message with mass vector $q$, the sign relevant to each private signal $r$ is the sign of $\sum_\theta q_\theta\psi(r\mid\theta)\Delta_R(\theta)$. At each fixed state the nonzero coefficients across private signals all have the sign of the common state-action difference $\Delta_R(\theta)$. The sender's actual tie choice is governed by expected sender utility at that private posterior. Weakly proposed best-response profiles can be used in the optimization because their assigned value is dominated by this actual payoff on ties.

## Closest earlier hardness

- Hossain et al. (ICML 2024), Theorem 3, considers a sender's best response against fixed schemes of other senders. This can be viewed as a state-conditional private channel. The proof in Appendix A.3, Figure 7, creates the action pairs $a_{j+},a_{j-}$ and a separate $a_\infty$, for $2k+1$ actions. The proof allows the two relevant payoff vectors to vary with the simulated type through these actions. It gives prior hardness for a more general action space. [Official proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hossain24c.html) · [Appendix A.3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/hossain24c/hossain24c.pdf#page=16)
- Dughmi and Xu (EC 2017), Theorem 6.2 in the extended arXiv version, proves hardness of public persuasion with many binary receivers, including no additive PTAS for average adoption with state-independent sender utility. In the hard instance the state equal to vertex $j$ gives receiver $j$ positive advantage and every other receiver negative advantage at that same state. This opposes the common-sign condition of the private likelihood model, so identifying types with receivers does not transfer the construction verbatim. [Dughmi and Xu, Theorem 6.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.06825#page=19)
- Castiglioni, Celli, Marchesi and Gatti (NeurIPS 2020), Definition 2 and Theorem 1, prove hardness of an offline uniform-known-type problem even with two actions. Its receiver utilities explicitly depend on type, and the type distribution concerns payoff functions rather than state-conditional evidence about a common payoff. Section 5's response-profile polytopes and posterior vertices give an earlier general method for the finite-support optimization step. This is a close conceptual precedent. [Online Bayesian Persuasion, pp. 5–6](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/ba5451d3c91a0f982f103cdbe249bc78-Paper.pdf#page=5)

## Related restricted or different models

Kamenica and Gentzkow describe the receiver-private-signal posterior update in Section IV.A and equation (3). Their Section IV.A also distinguishes a private parameter unrelated to the state. Kolotilin, Mylovanov, Zapechelnyuk and Li characterize some optimal experiments and type-reporting mechanisms in ordered linear environments. Those structural assumptions differ from an arbitrary rational private-information channel. Castiglioni, Marchesi and Gatti's type-reporting paper changes the allowed signal protocol through a menu of schemes. Xu's fixed-state public-persuasion algorithms and bicriteria approximation relaxations likewise address different axes of complexity. If the private alphabet of the present model has fixed size $k$, enumerating its $2^k$ binary action profiles and using one mass vector per profile gives a polynomial-sized LP. [Kamenica and Gentzkow](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.101.6.2590) · [Kolotilin et al.](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA13251) · [Type-reporting mechanisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00605) · [Xu (SODA 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07359)

The search covered the source versions, earlier cited works, and queries using private information, privately informed receiver, known channel, binary actions, exact optimization, state-independent payoff, hardness, and subsequent work by title and author. No direct theorem for the exact shared-payoff, known-channel, rational binary-action model appeared in the inspected sources. The present additive gap rules out a deterministic FPTAS; it makes no claim about a PTAS. The reduction uses the sender-favorable tie convention in its guards.
