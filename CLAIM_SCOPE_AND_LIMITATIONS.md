# Claim scope

- The decision problem uses a finite full-support prior, rational utilities and a known rational receiver channel. The sender's message and the receiver's private signal are conditionally independent given the state.
- The receiver has two actions. The private-signal count grows in the hard family. A fixed private alphabet gives a polynomial-sized action-profile program.
- Receiver ties favor the sender, and balanced guards in the reduction use this rule. The source's online exploration assumptions and stricter margins lie outside the theorem.
- Hardness holds for sender payoff equal to the chosen action in every state. The additive gap rules out a deterministic FPTAS unless P = NP. A constant-error hardness claim or PTAS exclusion is outside the result.
